## Bishkek

The journey begins in the Kyrgyz capital, a Soviet-planned city backed by the snow-capped Tian Shan mountains. Wide boulevards lined with oak trees give way to bustling bazaars where the scent of freshly baked bread mingles with the smoke of grilling shashlik.

## Karakol

A week into the journey, this former Russian garrison town serves as the gateway to the mountains. The old wooden Orthodox church, built entirely without nails, stands as a testament to the town's imperial past.

## Tosor

A small village on the southern shore of Issyk-Kul, the world's second-largest alpine lake. The waters never freeze, earning it the name "warm lake" in Kyrgyz.

## Kochkor

The heart of Kyrgyz felt-making traditions. Local women demonstrate the ancient art of shyrdak, crafting vibrant felt carpets using techniques passed down through generations of nomadic families.

## Song Kol

At 3,016 meters, this high-altitude lake is surrounded by summer pastures where nomadic herders still spend the season in their yurts. Horsemen gallop across the grasslands, and the night sky reveals more stars than seem possible.

## Jalal-Abad

Descending from the highlands into the fertile Fergana Valley, the air grows warmer and the landscape shifts to orchards and cotton fields. Ancient trading routes once converged here along the Silk Road.

## Arslanbob

Hidden in the mountains, the world's largest natural walnut forest blankets the slopes. In autumn, the entire village turns out to harvest nuts, carrying them down on donkeys along paths worn smooth by centuries of footsteps.

## Osh

One of the oldest cities in Central Asia, possibly founded 3,000 years ago. The great bazaar sprawls at the foot of Sulaiman-Too, a sacred mountain that has drawn pilgrims for millennia.

## Tulpar Lake

In the shadow of Peak Lenin, this remote lake reflects the massive pyramid of ice that rises to 7,134 meters. Base camp tents dot the moraine, and the silence is broken only by the crack of distant avalanches.

## Karakul

Crossing into Tajikistan and onto the Pamir Highway proper. This crater lake sits at 3,900 meters, surrounded by barren peaks. The thin air and stark beauty mark the beginning of the "Roof of the World."

## Murghab

The highest town in the former Soviet Union, a collection of shipping containers and mud-brick houses at 3,650 meters. The weekly bazaar brings traders from across the Pamirs and even from China.

## Dushanbe

The journey ends in the Tajik capital, where tree-lined avenues and Soviet monuments give way to the chaos of the main bazaar. After weeks in the mountains, the warmth and greenery feel almost tropical.
