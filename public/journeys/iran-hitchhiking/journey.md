# Tehran

## First Impressions

The plane descends into Tehran at night. Eighteen million people sprawl beneath, lights stretching to the mountains. Everything I've been told about Iran—the warnings, the politics, the fear—evaporates the moment I clear customs.

A Couchsurfing host picks me up. Within an hour, I'm sitting in his apartment drinking tea, eating fruit, trying to process the warmth.

![Tehran cityscape](/journeys/iran-hitchhiking/img-tehran.jpg)

"You came to Iran?" He seems genuinely surprised. "Most foreigners are afraid."

I tell him that's exactly why I came.

![With my Tehran host](/journeys/iran-hitchhiking/img-tehran-cser.jpg)

The next days blur together. Bazaars. Mountains. Traffic that defies physics. Everyone wants to talk. Where are you from? What do you think of Iran? The questions come with genuine curiosity, not suspicion.

# Isfahan

## Half the World

"Isfahan is half the world," the Persian saying goes. Standing in Naqsh-e Jahan Square, I understand. The scale is staggering—one of the largest squares on Earth, surrounded by mosques and palaces that took generations to build.

![Isfahan architecture](/journeys/iran-hitchhiking/img-isfahan.jpg)

My host here runs a small business in the gold market. He insists I stay a week. I insist I can only stay three days. We compromise on five.

![In the Isfahan bazaar](/journeys/iran-hitchhiking/img-isfahan-gold-market-cser.jpg)

Every evening, we walk the bridges over the Zayandeh River. Families picnic on the banks. Young people play music. The mood is lighter than anything I expected.

![With my Isfahan host](/journeys/iran-hitchhiking/img-isfahan-host.jpg)

"The news shows you one Iran," he says. "This is the other Iran. The real one."

# Yazd

## City of Wind Towers

Yazd rises from the desert like a mirage. The old city is a labyrinth of mud-brick walls, wind towers catching any breeze, Zoroastrian fire temples where flames have burned for centuries.

![The old city](/journeys/iran-hitchhiking/img-old-city.jpg)

I hitchhike here from Isfahan. Three rides, each one ending with an invitation to lunch, to tea, to stay the night. I accept the tea, decline the rest—I need to keep moving.

![Walking the desert](/journeys/iran-hitchhiking/img-walking-the-desert.jpg)

The desert outside Yazd is one of the hottest places on Earth. I visit with a local who knows the back roads. We find a spot where people have built underground shelters just to escape the heat.

![The hottest place](/journeys/iran-hitchhiking/img-hottest-place.jpg)

![Underground refuge](/journeys/iran-hitchhiking/img-hottest-place-underground.jpg)

At night, we camp in the dunes. The silence is absolute. No light pollution. No sound but wind. The stars are overwhelming.

![Desert camping](/journeys/iran-hitchhiking/img-desert-camping.jpg)

# Shiraz

## Gardens and Poetry

Shiraz is gentler than the rest of Iran. The city of poets—Hafez and Saadi are buried here, their tombs still pilgrimage sites. Gardens everywhere, roses and orange trees, fountains catching the light.

![Nasir ol-Molk Mosque](/journeys/iran-hitchhiking/img-shiraz-mosque.jpg)

The Pink Mosque lives up to every photograph. Morning light through stained glass, colors splashing across carpets, tourists and worshippers sharing the same awe.

![Morning light](/journeys/iran-hitchhiking/img-shiraz-mosque-2.jpg)

![Colors on the carpet](/journeys/iran-hitchhiking/img-shiraz-mosque-3.jpg)

My Couchsurfing host here is a university student. She wants to practice English. I want to understand Iran. Fair trade.

![With my Shiraz host](/journeys/iran-hitchhiking/img-shiraz-cser.jpg)

"The hardest thing," she says, "is that the world thinks we are our government. We are not our government."

![No beer, but endless tea](/journeys/iran-hitchhiking/img-no-beer.jpg)

# Persepolis

## The Empire's Bones

Persepolis was the ceremonial capital of the Achaemenid Empire—Cyrus, Darius, Xerxes. Alexander burned it in 330 BC. What remains is still staggering.

![The ruins](/journeys/iran-hitchhiking/img-persepolis.jpg)

I spend hours wandering the columns and reliefs. Delegations from across the ancient world carved in stone, bringing tribute to the King of Kings. Lions and bulls. Winged figures. A civilization that stretched from Egypt to India.

![Details in stone](/journeys/iran-hitchhiking/img-persepolis-2.jpg)

The hospitality continues even here. A family invites me to share their picnic lunch among the ruins. We can barely communicate, but the food speaks clearly: rice, kebab, yogurt, fresh bread.

![Iranian hospitality](/journeys/iran-hitchhiking/img-hospitality.jpg)

![More hospitality](/journeys/iran-hitchhiking/img-hospitality-2.jpg)

![And more](/journeys/iran-hitchhiking/img-hospitality-3.jpg)

---

Three weeks of thumbs up and open doors. The math doesn't make sense: a country under sanctions, isolated by politics, yet more welcoming than anywhere I've been.

Every preconception shattered. Every warning proved wrong. Iran taught me to trust my own eyes over headlines.
